# See You Later? (Name is work in progress) We all need to go out more?

## What and Why?

In between text messages with our friends, there are hundreds of links scattered that lead to places we are dying to go to. They could be a walk down the block or as far as the little island of Tuvalu. The point is, even after sending each other all these suggestions, we always end up at our regular spot, completely forgetting about the place we wanted to go to, and then complaining that we do the same thing every time.

Planning trips is even more incoherent. We have reels saved from months ago about the thrift store in NYC that we mutually agreed was a must-go when we landed, but it's week three of classes and we've barely left Manhattan. And it's way too many taps to open Google Maps, type the name of the place, drop a pin and save it, especially when we're in the mood of doomscrolling. So the links pile up in the chat and nothing happens. The place never gets saved anywhere you'd actually look again, and even if it does get saved, nobody knows when to go, or if we should go at all.

This is where Later comes in. When you see a cheap-eats restaurant you're dying to try, you paste the link into the app and we do the rest: find the place, drop it on the map of that city, and put it in front of your friends. Then your friends say whether they're in.

Existing apps like Google Maps and Wanderlog are built for Type A people, organized enough to search for places themselves and keep them in lists. They assume you're already in planning mode, when the moment you actually find a place while scrolling is the moment you're least willing to do work. And none of them have an opinion about the fact that six people disagree about whether the 90-minute hike is happening.

## For Whom?

The first users we have in mind are study-away students. Students on a semester abroad have the sharpest version of this problem: a few months in a city they don't know, a friend group that moves in packs, and a hard deadline before they leave. They save places constantly and visit almost none of them when stuck between assignments and exams. As study-away students ourselves, we already have chatlogs full of unvisited links to prove this problem exists.

Past that, this is for the people who send each other way too many reels of places to go and then become listless when the free time to go actually arrives. Anyone who has spent a Friday evening in a group chat where five people say "I'm down for whatever" and nobody leaves the apartment is the target user.

## How?

Here's the flow from a user's side.

You're scrolling TikTok and a video comes up about a noodle shop in the East Village. You hit share, copy link, and paste it into Later. The app reads the post, identifies the restaurant, and adds it to your New York list with a pin on the map. If the app can't work out the place from the post, it suggests options based on what it did read, so confirming the right one is a single tap.

Your friends on that list see the noodle shop appear. Each of them marks it: must-go, down for it, or hard pass, and can leave a note explaining ("their vegetarian options are bad" or "it's a 40 min walk from the subway"). The map shows what the group actually wants, so the places three people called must-go rise to the top and the one nobody voted for stops taking up space. On Friday night, instead of the group chat going in circles, you open the map, look at what's near you and what everybody already agreed on, and go.

Lists are organized by city. A pin from a New York restaurant lands in the New York list; a pin from Abu Dhabi lands in the Abu Dhabi list. Each list has its own set of invited friends, so your NYC list can have your study-away friends and your Abu Dhabi list can have people back on campus.

### Product Backlog

- Profiles, so you can find and invite friends by username.
- City lists you create and invite people into.
- Paste-a-link saving, with automatic place identification and a fallback to search when identification fails.
- A shared map and list view per city, with places categorized (food, shopping, viewpoint) and open to notes.
- Group voting on each place, with the map surfacing what the group actually wants to do.
- A view of what's most-pinned across your friends, so you can see what everyone is saving.

### Deliberately not included

- **Reviews and ratings.** Too cluttered, linking back to Google Maps handles it. "Three of your friends saved this" is more useful to you than a stranger's star rating.
- **Bookings, tickets, or payments.** 
- **Itinerary scheduling by time of day.** Deciding what the group wants is the hard part, and we wouldn't want to remove the social element of finding each other's free time.
- **A native iOS/Android app.** Mobile web only.

### Possible later additions / stretch goals

- Recommendations based on what you've pinned, including travel style, so the app can suggest places the group would likely agree on.
- Surfacing "locals love this spot" for places that get saved a lot in a given city.
